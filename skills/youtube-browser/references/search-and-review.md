# Search selection and video review

## Search for the user's outcome

For broad topic research, use [batch-research.md](batch-research.md), which adds a planned multi-query acquisition and screening funnel. For a quick lookup, make the query specific to the question, including model, software version, date, or intended task when known, and begin with one focused search. When the results reveal a concrete terminology mismatch, make a targeted refinement through the visible search UI rather than running query grids. Follow the active browser's lookup guidance for any external search fallback.

Choose the result type intentionally. A short demonstration may answer whether a feature exists; a detailed procedure may require a longer walkthrough. An app-based feature demonstration and a key-fob configuration tutorial can share keywords while answering different questions. Treat sponsored results as advertising, and recommendations as discovery leads rather than verified matches.

Use visible YouTube filters when useful, such as upload date, duration, type, and subtitles. Observe the menu first and verify what was selected; do not memorize encoded filter parameters. A subtitle filter does not establish accurate captions. Relative upload dates are approximate until the expanded watch page reveals an exact date.

For each shortlisted video, capture only useful selection evidence:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Title, channel, canonical URL | Identify the actual candidate without tracking parameters. |
| Published date and duration | Assess freshness and expected depth. |
| Topic/model/version match | Explain why it answers this request. |
| Evidence demonstrated | Distinguish walkthrough, assertion, promotion, and direct test. |
| Inspection coverage | State metadata-only, transcript read, scenes reviewed, or audio inspected. |
| Limitations | Identify missing steps, compatibility gaps, sponsorship, or unresolved claims. |

Rank by fit and supported usefulness. A newer video can still be for the wrong platform; an older primary-source demonstration can be more useful. A search result being first, popular, or heavily liked does not independently establish truth. Avoid numeric scores unless they add value and the criteria are explained.

## Choose the analysis depth

- **Find/recommend:** Verify candidate identity and fit on the watch page. Inspect enough actual content to support each recommendation. Do not imply complete viewing of all candidates.
- **Summarize/explain:** Read the available transcript, map the argument or process, and inspect scenes when visual evidence matters. Scan a long transcript in contiguous chunks so omitted middle sections do not become invented coverage.
- **Describe verbosely:** Follow the actual chronology. Explain the person's actions, tools, decisions, problems, reported waiting periods, and observed result. Mention a later plan as a plan, and an off-camera activity as something the person reports. Do not convert an incomplete demonstration into a universal instruction manual.
- **Technical review/fact-check:** Inspect screen labels, values, output messages, and demonstrated results around important moments. Compare material claims with original manufacturer/project documentation. Separate observed evidence, speaker assertion, and inference.
- **Creative critique:** Assess the opening promise, structure, clarity, demonstration, and payoff in relation to the intended audience. Describe specific fixes. Audio and motion judgments need those modalities; captions plus sparse frames support a narrower critique.
- **Compare:** Apply the same criteria and explain uneven inspection coverage. Avoid letting a strong thumbnail outweigh substantive evidence or assuming videos with matching titles demonstrate the same procedure.

## Resolve contradictions

When narration and the visible result conflict, inspect the scene and nearby context before deciding what happened. For example, a speaker can declare a procedure successful while a screen shows a prerequisite failure. Describe both and explain which conclusion the observed evidence supports. Do not claim the whole procedure failed merely because one displayed test failed.

Check automatic-caption errors in names, settings, units, and negation against visible text or original documentation. For a precise spoken quotation, consult actual audio if available; otherwise identify it as a caption-derived approximation and paraphrase. A caption export does not reveal tone, music quality, or whether a speaker pronounced a term incorrectly.

Preserve original source timestamps despite ads, sponsor skips, trimmed downloads, or playback speed. A comment timestamp is a lead; verify the linked scene before relying on it. Comments can surface questions or reported experiences, but are neither a representative audience survey nor proof that a technical claim is true. Keep any quoted viewer material brief and omit irrelevant personal identifiers.

## Finish with useful evidence

Match length to the user's request. Answer first, then give the findings and necessary caveats. Use canonical links such as `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID` and timestamp links such as `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=135s`, using actual IDs/times.

State whether the answer used browser page details, a manual or automatic transcript, sampled scenes, actual audio, yt-dlp evidence, or outside sources. Distinguish inaccessible evidence from absent content. Do not present export/download success as analysis completion or imply a full watch when only selected ranges were inspected.
